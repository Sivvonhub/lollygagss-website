What Is BIAB?
Why BIAB Nails Are So Popular in Bali Right Now

If you have spent any time looking at nail studios in Bali recently, you have probably seen the term BIAB everywhere. Here is what it actually means, why it works so well in the tropics, and whether it is right for you.

By Lina Zahirah Dimes
Studio Teuku Umar, Kerobokan
Published May 2026

Clients coming in from Seminyak, Canggu, Kuta, and Kerobokan ask about BIAB almost every week. Some already know it from Australia or the UK and are relieved to find it properly done in Bali. Others have seen it on Instagram and want to understand what all the fuss is about before booking.

At Lollygagss Studio near Teuku Umar, BIAB has become one of our most requested nail services, and not just because it looks beautiful. It genuinely performs better in Bali's climate than almost anything else we offer.

What does BIAB stand for?

BIAB stands for Builder In A Bottle. It is a thick, strengthening builder gel that is applied directly over your natural nails, not as an extension, but as a protective and supportive layer that encourages healthy growth.

Unlike a standard gel manicure, which adds colour and a bit of shine with very little structural benefit, BIAB adds real strength to the nail plate. It helps prevent the bending, tearing, and peeling that most people experience here in Bali, where the combination of heat, humidity, salt water, and sunscreen can be genuinely harsh on nails.

"BIAB changed how I think about nail maintenance. I stopped treating it as a one-off and started thinking about it the way I think about skincare, something consistent that actually builds results over time."

Lina Zahirah Dimes, Lollygagss Studio

Why is BIAB so popular in Bali specifically?

There is a particular kind of nail damage that happens when you live in Bali, or spend a long enough holiday here. The heat draws moisture from the nail bed. Pool chlorine strips the nail's natural oils. Salt water from Kuta or Seminyak beach softens the nail plate over time. And if you are applying SPF every day, which you should be, that sunscreen contact is slowly working against your gel adhesion.

Standard gel polish is not really designed to cope with all of that simultaneously. BIAB is. The builder formula creates a flexible, bonded layer that moves slightly with the nail rather than cracking under stress. That flexibility is what gives BIAB its reputation for longevity in tropical climates.

Clients who come to us from Legian, Denpasar, and the villas around Kerobokan frequently tell us they have been bouncing between studios trying to make their gel last more than two weeks. Nine times out of ten, BIAB solves the problem entirely.

Is BIAB better than a gel manicure?

It depends entirely on what you want your nails to do. A regular gel manicure is still a beautiful option, it gives you colour, shine, and a polished finish, but it does not do much for the structural health of the nail itself. If you are trying to decide between the two, or wondering where extensions fit in, we have put together an honest guide with gel, BIAB, and extensions compared.

BIAB is the better choice when you want your nails to actually grow. The builder layer acts almost like a scaffold: it protects what is underneath, supports the free edge, and gives weak or damaged nails somewhere stable to grow from. That is why clients who have previously broken nails every few weeks often find that after a few consistent BIAB appointments, their natural nails are noticeably longer and healthier.

How long does BIAB last in Bali?

Most BIAB clients at our studio come back every three to four weeks for a fresh set, which keeps the structure balanced as the nail grows and avoids the lifting you sometimes see with gel manicures left too long.

That said, lifestyle matters. If you are spending every morning at the beach in Kuta or Legian, afternoons in the pool, and evenings with SPF still on your hands, your retention will be shorter than someone in an office in Denpasar. The nails are not failing, they are just working harder.

Lina's approach at the studio is to look at what your nails have actually been through before deciding how to prep them. A client coming in from Canggu who has been surfing will need a different prep approach than someone who works from home and is booking a treat. That context changes everything about how the product is applied and how long it will perform.

What to expect from your first BIAB appointment

A BIAB Manicure at Lollygagss takes around 90 minutes. Lina does all the work herself, there is no rotation between technicians, no one passing you off to a junior staff member halfway through. You get the same person from prep to finish.

The process involves shaping and prepping the natural nail, applying a bonding base, then building the BIAB layer and curing it under UV. From there you choose your colour, or keep it clear for a clean strengthening overlay. It finishes with cuticle care and a nail vitamin.

First-timers sometimes worry that BIAB will look thick or unnatural. In practice, it sits close to the nail and looks far more natural than acrylic or most hard gels. Most clients leave feeling like their hands look better than they have in years.

How to make your BIAB last longer in Bali

  • Apply cuticle oil once or twice a day, particularly important in Bali's dry air conditioning.
  • Avoid prolonged soaking in the first 24 hours after your appointment.
  • Use gloves for cleaning or any heavy product exposure where you can.
  • Rinse your hands after swimming in the ocean or pool, rather than letting salt or chlorine sit on the nail edge.
  • Book your next appointment at three weeks rather than waiting until there is obvious lifting.
  • Do not pick or peel, if something feels off, message us on WhatsApp and Lina can advise.
